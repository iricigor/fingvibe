    Understand device results¶

    Devices is a persistent inventory: a saved list of known devices. It includes local observations and devices imported from Fing. Unused IP addresses do not become device records.

    Device status¶

    Status Meaning
    Online The device responded to ping or ARP in its latest applicable scan evidence, including saved results.
    Offline A previously observed device did not respond in a later successful scan of the same identified network and subnet.
    Unknown No suitable local scan status is available. Restart alone does not make a known status Unknown.
    Fing Only The imported MAC address has never been observed in a local FingVibe scan.

    Offline is not proof of power-off. A device may be sleeping, blocking probes, or outside the scan's reachable network. After restart, saved Online/Offline statuses remain available, with their evidence age. Starting a scan retains unresolved statuses; fresh responders become Online, but only full successful completion can make nonresponders Offline on that same network. Cancellation or failure retains prior statuses and committed positive results. Online is scan-relative, not proof of continuous live presence.

    Color supports the status text. Green, gray, or amber alone is not the meaning. The totals describe the whole inventory, even when filters hide some rows.

    Search and filter¶

    1. Enter a name, address, vendor, or other displayed information in Search devices.
    2. Open Status to choose one or more statuses.
    3. Choose the funnel icon to show more filters.
    4. Select values in Device type, Operating system, Vendor, Person, Room, Ports, or Usage category.
    5. Choose Clear filters beside the funnel to remove all restrictions.

    Selecting several values within one category means "any of these." Different categories combine: a device must match each selected category and the search text. An empty selection means no restriction.

    For example, select Online, a room, and With open ports to see responding devices in that room with observed services. Unassigned finds devices with no person or room. Unknown type or OS finds devices without that classification.

    Hiding the second filter row does not clear it. The funnel count shows active secondary categories. Status is remembered after restart; search and the other categories are not.

    Any means any known value, not every value. It appears immediately after Unknown, Unassigned or Unclassified when that dropdown offers at least two known choices. Selecting Any replaces individual choices; selecting an individual choice replaces Any. Clearing the category still includes everything. For Person/Room, Any means assigned; for Usage category, it excludes Unclassified. For Vendor, it includes identified manufacturers and excludes both Unknown and Randomized MAC. The threshold counts dropdown choices, not devices. An active Any filter keeps its meaning when new values appear. Status remembers Any after restart just as it remembers individual statuses.

    The sample search matches no device. It does not mean the inventory is empty. Screenshot sources.

    Usage category¶

    The category uses one line. A small pin marks a manual override; an information indicator marks a limited automatic estimate or unavailable evidence. Unclassified is visually quieter, not a confirmed category or an error. Hover for an explanation, or focus the category button and press Enter/Space to open its details. They include the source, reason, eligible scan counts, observation period and missing evidence. Loading and Unavailable remain explicit; no eligible scans never produces a made-up percentage.

    The column does not add a second row of repeated source text or change the classification rules. See History and usage categories for the rules and manual overrides.

    Sort and resize¶

    Choose a column heading to sort ascending. Choose it again to reverse the order. Combined columns keep separate heading actions for their fields. Person and Room sort by full name, not by the short label.

    Drag a column boundary to resize it. Use Settings to show, hide, or combine columns. Name and IP address always remain visible. The final visible column keeps a right-edge separator and resize handle. Space beyond it is unused table space, not an automatically enlarged column.

    Response times and service icons¶

    Response time is the measured ping delay in milliseconds (ms). A smaller value means that ping returned faster, not that the device's internet connection is faster. The response bars share one scale for the displayed scan. Filtering does not change that scale.

    Service icons represent observed ports, such as a web or file-sharing service. Hover over an icon for the port number and name. An open port is not a security verdict. It does not prove a service is safe, unsafe, or running a particular operating system.

    Last seen¶

    Last seen shows the latest local presence evidence for the selected network. Online in that column describes the displayed scan. A relative age, such as 2 h ago, describes saved evidence.

    Never means an imported-only device has not been seen by a local scan. Unavailable means suitable evidence is missing or cannot be read. Hover or focus the value to see its exact time or explanation. Age text refreshes while Devices is open; it does not run another scan.

    Private and virtual devices¶

    Randomized MAC is the display label for a locally administered MAC address. It is not a known manufacturer and does not prove random generation or rotation: the address may also have been assigned manually. Its explanation states this limitation.

    Unknown means FingVibe has not identified the manufacturer. This also covers a raw IEEE registry entry of Private; the explanation preserves that distinction without claiming that other information cannot identify the device. Saved manufacturer corrections and imported brands still take precedence.

    A multiple-PC icon can mark a virtual device: FingVibe's inferred identity for certain sparse private-prefix observations across changing MAC addresses. It does not mean a virtual machine. The association is an estimate, not proof that several observations belong to one physical device. See Device details.
